# Rhetorical Diagnosis: Logos, Ethos, Pathos, Kairos

Use these concepts as a **secondary diagnostic layer**. Do not add them again to the 100-point score.

## Logos

**Core question:** Does this make sense?

Inspect:
- claim–evidence fit;
- causal reasoning;
- internal consistency;
- distinctions and definitions;
- missing inferential steps;
- counterexamples.

Logos is especially important in Expert, Explainer, Persuasive, and Practical writing.

## Ethos

**Core question:** Why should the reader trust this voice or judgment?

Ethos is not merely credentials. It can be built through:
- intellectual honesty;
- accurate sourcing;
- admitting limits;
- fair representation of opposing views;
- consistency;
- restraint;
- domain competence.

In Expert writing, Ethos often appears as **epistemic discipline**: saying only what the evidence supports.

## Pathos

**Core question:** Why should the reader care or feel the stakes?

Pathos is not synonymous with sentimentality.

It can include:
- curiosity;
- concern;
- urgency;
- empathy;
- anger;
- hope;
- wonder;
- identification.

In Literary writing, Pathos is often central. In Explainer writing, it helps sustain attention and importance. In Persuasive writing, it shapes judgment and motivation.

## Kairos

**Core question:** Is this the right message, for this audience, in this situation, at this time?

Inspect:
- timing;
- genre expectations;
- reader knowledge;
- social context;
- urgency;
- medium;
- what can reasonably be asked of the reader now.

Kairos is central to Persuasive and Practical writing and often important in Explainer writing.

## Relative importance by mode

| Mode | Logos | Ethos | Pathos | Kairos |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Literary | ★★ | ★★★ | ★★★★★ | ★★★ |
| Expert | ★★★★★ | ★★★★★ | ★ | ★★★ |
| Explainer | ★★★★★ | ★★★★ | ★★★★ | ★★★★ |
| Persuasive | ★★★★★ | ★★★★★ | ★★★★★ | ★★★★★ |
| Practical | ★★★★ | ★★★ | ★★ | ★★★★★ |

These stars are not scores. They indicate typical diagnostic importance.

## Suggested diagnostic output

| Dimension | Level | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Logos | ★★★★☆ | Main causal chain is clear, but one inferential step is assumed |
| Ethos | ★★★★★ | Limits and opposing evidence are treated fairly |
| Pathos | ★★★☆☆ | Stakes are present but abstract |
| Kairos | ★★★★☆ | Well matched to audience, but call to action arrives late |

## Common misunderstandings

- High Pathos does not excuse weak evidence.
- Strong credentials do not automatically create strong Ethos.
- More logic does not always mean better Practical writing.
- Kairos is not just “timeliness”; it includes situational appropriateness.
