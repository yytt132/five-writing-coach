# Writing Taxonomy

## 1. Two complementary classification systems

Five Writing Coach uses two layers.

### Traditional four discourse modes

| Mode | Core question | Main function |
|---|---|---|
| Narration | What happened? | Arrange events through time |
| Description | What is it like? | Make a scene, object, or experience concrete |
| Exposition | What is it and how does it work? | Explain concepts and mechanisms |
| Argumentation | Why should this claim be accepted? | Support a claim with reasons |

These are best treated as **methods used inside a text**, not mutually exclusive genres.

### Five purpose-driven writing modes

| Mode | Intended change in the reader | Typical genres |
|---|---|---|
| Literary | Experience | Fiction, memoir, literary essay |
| Expert | Verify or judge a claim | Research paper, technical report |
| Explainer | Understand | Science journalism, textbook, popular nonfiction |
| Persuasive | Change judgment or action | Column, editorial, speech, proposal |
| Practical | Act correctly | Email, memo, notice, manual |

The central diagnostic question is:

> What should be different in the reader after reading this text?

## 2. Literary writing

**Primary goal:** create an experience rather than merely report information.

Typical strengths:
- scenes;
- sensory detail;
- voice;
- character;
- emotional movement;
- narrative tension.

Traditional modes most often used:
- narration;
- description.

## 3. Expert writing

**Primary goal:** make claims precise, testable, and appropriately qualified.

Typical strengths:
- definition;
- evidence;
- explicit reasoning;
- methodological transparency;
- uncertainty management;
- traceable sources.

Traditional modes most often used:
- exposition;
- argumentation.

## 4. Explainer writing

**Primary goal:** transform complexity into an accurate mental model for a non-specialist or less-specialized reader.

Typical strengths:
- known-to-unknown sequencing;
- examples and analogies;
- causal explanation;
- terminology introduced after intuition;
- information compression;
- reader-oriented structure.

Traditional modes most often used:
- exposition;
- narration and description as support.

### Science journalism

Science journalism is primarily Explainer writing:

> Explainer structure + Expert accuracy + Literary readability

It may become Persuasive when it explicitly argues for a judgment, policy, or action.

## 5. Persuasive writing

**Primary goal:** change what the reader believes, judges, prioritizes, or does.

Typical strengths:
- clear thesis;
- reasons and evidence;
- counterargument;
- concession and rebuttal;
- reader-value alignment;
- rhetorical timing.

Traditional mode most often used:
- argumentation.

### Argumentative columns

Argumentative columns are primarily Persuasive writing:

> Persuasive argument + Expert evidence + Explainer clarity + optional Literary devices

A strong column does not merely state a strong opinion. It survives the strongest plausible objection.

## 6. Practical writing

**Primary goal:** enable correct action with minimal friction.

Typical strengths:
- clear task;
- required information;
- dates and conditions;
- scannability;
- error prevention;
- concise wording.

Traditional mode most often used:
- exposition, often with procedural or directive structure.

## 7. Hybrid texts

Do not force texts into pure categories.

Examples:

- A science feature may be **Explainer primary**, **Expert + Literary supporting**.
- A policy op-ed may be **Persuasive primary**, **Expert + Explainer supporting**.
- A research abstract is **Expert primary**, with strong Practical constraints.
- A public-health notice may be **Practical primary**, with Explainer support.
- A memoir arguing for social reform may be **Persuasive primary** with heavy Literary support.

When scoring, the user-declared target mode has priority.
