# 100-Point Rubric

## A. Common criteria — 40 points

Score each 0–10.

| Criterion | Diagnostic question |
|---|---|
| Purpose fit | Does the text clearly achieve what it is trying to do? |
| Structure and flow | Do sentences, paragraphs, and sections form an intelligible progression? |
| Sentence clarity | Is meaning precise without needless complexity or ambiguity? |
| Audience fit | Does the text match the reader's knowledge, needs, and situation? |

## B. Mode-specific criteria — 60 points

### 1. Literary writing

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Scene | Can the reader picture or inhabit the situation? |
| Sensory concreteness | Are abstract claims embodied in specific details? |
| Voice / character | Is there a distinct human presence or perspective? |
| Emotional effect | Does the text create felt experience rather than merely label emotion? |
| Narrative tension | Is there movement, curiosity, conflict, or expectation? |
| Language distinctiveness | Does the prose avoid dead clichés and generic expression? |

### 2. Expert writing

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Conceptual accuracy | Are terms and distinctions used precisely? |
| Claim clarity | Is it clear exactly what is being claimed? |
| Evidence quality | Is the claim supported by appropriate evidence? |
| Logical connection | Does the conclusion follow from the premises or evidence? |
| Uncertainty and limits | Are caveats, alternatives, and scope handled honestly? |
| Verifiability | Can a reader trace sources, methods, definitions, or evidence? |

### 3. Explainer writing

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Core clarity | Can the reader restate the main idea after reading? |
| Explanation order | Does the text move from known to unknown and simple to complex? |
| Concept translation | Are technical ideas rendered understandable without distortion? |
| Examples and analogies | Are abstractions made concrete where useful? |
| Causal / relational explanation | Does the reader understand why or how, not just what? |
| Information compression | Are essentials preserved while avoidable complexity is removed? |

### 4. Persuasive writing

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Thesis clarity | Is the position unmistakable? |
| Strength of reasons / evidence | Are there compelling reasons to accept it? |
| Logical validity | Are there leaps, contradictions, or fallacies? |
| Counterargument handling | Are strong objections represented and answered fairly? |
| Reader perspective | Does the argument connect to the reader's values, stakes, or concerns? |
| Judgment / action direction | Is the desired conclusion or action clear? |

### 5. Practical writing

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Purpose clarity | Does the reader immediately know why the text exists? |
| Action clarity | Is it obvious what the reader must do? |
| Information completeness | Are dates, methods, conditions, locations, owners, and dependencies present as needed? |
| Findability | Can important information be located quickly? |
| Concision | Is unnecessary wording removed? |
| Error prevention | Does the text reduce the chance of misunderstanding or incorrect action? |

## C. Score anchors

Use the following anchors for each 0–10 criterion.

| Score | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 9–10 | Excellent; consistently performs the intended function |
| 7–8 | Strong; works well with limited weaknesses |
| 5–6 | Mixed; partly effective but inconsistent |
| 3–4 | Weak; important execution problems |
| 0–2 | Absent, contradictory, or actively harmful to the goal |

## D. Total-score anchors

| Total | Interpretation |
|---:|---|
| 90–100 | Exceptional for the stated purpose |
| 80–89 | Strong |
| 70–79 | Competent |
| 60–69 | Uneven |
| 50–59 | Weak fit or serious execution problems |
| <50 | Does not yet function effectively for the target purpose |

## E. Multi-mode scoring rule

When the same text is scored in all five modes:

- score each mode independently;
- do not average unless the user explicitly asks;
- do not penalize a text merely because it was not written for another mode;
- interpret cross-mode differences as a profile, not a contradiction.

Example:

A rhetorical essay may score:
- Literary 86
- Expert 67
- Explainer 76
- Persuasive 93
- Practical 52

This means the text is highly effective as persuasion, not that it is “inconsistently written.”
