# Fatal-Error Checks

A fatal error is not an ordinary weakness. It is a failure that can invalidate the text's primary purpose even when the prose is otherwise strong.

Report fatal errors separately from the 100-point score.

## General fatal errors

- The text's declared conclusion contradicts its own evidence.
- The key action, claim, or referent is so ambiguous that the primary purpose cannot be completed.
- A crucial fact is fabricated or silently invented when the text depends on factual reliability.
- The text attributes a source, quote, or result in a way that materially misrepresents it.

## Literary writing

Potential fatal errors:
- no coherent point of view where one is required for comprehension;
- timeline or identity confusion that is clearly accidental and breaks the narrative;
- emotional labeling replaces all concrete experience in a piece whose goal is immersive scene.

Do not call stylistic preference a fatal error.

## Expert writing

Potential fatal errors:
- evidence does not support the central claim;
- correlation is presented as causation without justification;
- critical source, method, or denominator is missing so the result cannot be interpreted;
- uncertainty is suppressed in a way that materially changes the conclusion;
- key terms shift meaning across the text.

## Explainer writing

Potential fatal errors:
- simplification creates a materially false mental model;
- analogy reverses or distorts the mechanism;
- necessary prerequisite knowledge is omitted so the explanation becomes misleading;
- a probabilistic relationship is explained as deterministic.

## Persuasive writing

Potential fatal errors:
- central argument depends on a demonstrably false premise;
- the text attacks a caricature instead of the real opposing position;
- conclusion does not follow from the reasons given;
- emotional pressure substitutes for the evidence needed by the claim.

## Practical writing

Potential fatal errors:
- wrong date, address, amount, deadline, recipient, or action;
- a required step is omitted;
- instructions contain mutually incompatible steps;
- wording creates a realistic risk of the reader doing the wrong thing.

## Reporting rule

Use:

> Fatal errors: none found.

or:

> Fatal errors: 1  
> - The explanation treats an association as a proven causal mechanism, which changes the reader's mental model.

Keep the list small and concrete.
